Combat State Diagram
Below is a text‑based representation of the combat state machine for Kai and Jax. Each state represents a discrete posture or action; transitions are triggered by player inputs, environmental conditions or AI responses. The diagram is intentionally lean, avoiding redundant states.
State	Key Triggers	Valid Transitions
Idle	no input, or post‑action recovery	walk, run, jump, dash, light attack, heavy attack, parry
Walk	analog/joystick input below run threshold	idle, run, jump, dash, light attack, parry
Run	sustained full analog input	walk, dash, jump, heavy attack, parry
Dash	swipe or dash button	idle (after recovery), attack cancel (during recovery window)
Jump	upward swipe or button	aerial attack, air dodge, slam (Kai), juggle (Jax), land
Light Attack	tap attack button	combo sequence (max chain length), special, cancel to parry
Heavy Attack	long press attack button	slam (Kai), launch (Jax), follow‑up heavy, cancel to parry
Special	context‑sensitive tail or ability	idle, combo follow‑up, parry
Parry	timed press (context‑sensitive)	riposte, dodge, idle
Hurt	received damage, minor stagger	idle, run (if recovery movement), knockdown (if threshold met)
Knockdown	received high damage or failed parry	get‑up, roll, switch character (in multiplayer)
Fusion	bond and health conditions met	special fusion moves, revert to idle when duration expires
State flow summary:
Idle → Walk/Run occurs on analog input. Run → Dash is available via a separate button or a double tap.
Attack chains flow from Light Attack into follow‑ups until stamina or chain length limits. Heavy attacks cancel light chains when the player holds the button.
Parry is a universal interrupt: pressing parry during most attack or movement states cancels into the parry state, consuming stamina.
Hurt → Knockdown triggers when damage exceeds a threshold or when a parry fails disastrously.
Fusion is only accessible when both characters meet the bond and health conditions; exiting Fusion drops back to Idle with a cooldown.
Malakor Boss Fight Script
Malakor serves as the benchmark encounter that teaches players to respect timing, spacing and adaptability. The fight unfolds across four escalating phases. Each phase has its own set of attacks and behavioural rules.
Phase 1 – Assessment
Malakor opens conservatively, testing the players’ reactions.
Attacks: slow, telegraphed swings; a lunging grab; ground shockwave that travels in a straight line.
Behaviour: he observes for patterns, intentionally leaving windows for parries. Players who spam attacks will be punished with a grab.
Teaching point: learn to parry and dodge the obvious tells. Greedy combos are punished.
Phase 2 – Domination
Once half his armour is broken, Malakor increases his aggression.
Attacks: faster combos with delayed swings; sweeping tail attacks that hit in an arc; forced separation of Kai and Jax via targeted charges.
Behaviour: he begins to counter light attack spam by armour‑breaking on the third hit. He attempts to isolate one character, forcing players to use bond rescues.
Teaching point: mix up your attacks, pay attention to spacing, and use bond mechanics to reunite the duo.
Phase 3 – Rage
Malakor enters a berserk state when his health drops below 40 %, amplifying his offence.
Attacks: area‑denial fields that force relocation; ground eruptions on missed dodges; unblockable overhead slams.
Behaviour: he staggers only after perfect parries or heavy‑attack counters. His moves chain unpredictably, encouraging patience and observation.
Teaching point: master timing windows for parry vs dodge. Recognize when to disengage rather than chase damage.
Phase 4 – Collapse
With his armour shattered and health critical, Malakor becomes slower but extremely dangerous.
Attacks: a devastating beam that tracks one player; desperate grab attempts with extended range; arena collapse events that limit space.
Behaviour: he telegraphs his ultimate attack with a long wind‑up. Successful counters stagger him for a fusion finisher opportunity.
Teaching point: pay attention to audio/visual cues. This phase tests everything learned so far: parry timing, positioning, bond usage, and patience.
Defeating Malakor provides a narrative payoff and unlocks new tail modules for both characters.
Mobile Control Layout
Mobile controls must be intuitive and responsive, never feeling like a desktop UI forced onto a touchscreen. The design emphasises thumb‑zone ergonomics and context‑sensitive buttons.
Key components:

Left thumb area (bottom‑left quarter of the screen) hosts a virtual joystick. The joystick supports 360° movement, with a run threshold when the thumb reaches the outer ring.
Right thumb area contains three fixed action buttons:
Light Attack – a small circular button for quick strikes.
Heavy/Special – a larger button above light attack; long press triggers heavy, while short tap triggers a context special.
Dodge/Parry – positioned slightly below the other buttons. Swiping in this area triggers a dodge; a timed tap triggers a parry.
Vertical swipe up anywhere on the right side triggers a jump. Swiping down could be reserved for quick drop or dive attacks.
Top HUD bar displays health, stamina, and bond meter. It remains minimal and transparent during combat.
Context buttons appear only when relevant (e.g., prompt to rescue partner when Jax is grabbed).
The layout in the image reflects these principles, using pastel colours and clean shapes to avoid screen clutter.
Progression Economy Rules
Progression in this game is designed to reward mastery and exploration rather than grind. The systems are simple, clear, and interconnected.
Tail Modules: each tail grants a passive bonus (e.g., faster dash recovery), an active move (e.g., Kai’s grapple, Jax’s shadow leap), and a traversal permission (e.g., unlocks wall‑climb surfaces). Tails are unlocked through story progression and optional challenges, not random drops.
Relics: equipped items that modify combat style rather than stats. Examples include a relic that converts perfect parries into area‑of‑effect knockbacks, or one that lengthens combo windows at the cost of stamina. Relics are mutually exclusive within their category, encouraging meaningful builds.
Aether Shards: currency earned by rescuing survivors and completing rift challenges. Shards upgrade tail modules and relics, unlocking new behaviours (not numerical upgrades). There is no loot‑box or pay‑to‑win mechanic.
Bond Meter: builds by cooperative actions (assist strikes, rescues, synchronized dodges) and unlocks fusion when full. There is no experience bar; the bond meter itself acts as a short‑term progression loop within a mission.
Story Gates: major abilities (like Fusion) and new zones are gated by story progression, not level grind. Side missions provide optional relics and narrative bits but are never mandatory for core progression.
